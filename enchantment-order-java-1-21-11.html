Choose an efficient Java enchantment order

Compare valid anvil orders rather than assuming that merging every book first is cheapest. The BlockWise optimizer searches the legal orders it models and reports the smallest total of displayed level costs, not necessarily the fewest raw XP points.

Why order matters

Each anvil operation can add prior work to the resulting item or book. A fresh item has a penalty of zero; successive work can produce penalties of 1, 3, and 7. Both inputs' prior work can affect a later operation. Book enchantments also have different costs, and swapping which book is sacrificed can change the cost.

Worked comparison

Two fresh books can be applied directly in two operations. If their individual enchantment charges are A and B, applying A first costs A, then B plus the item's one-level prior-work penalty: A + B + 1. Combining the books first adds a separate book-merge charge before applying the resulting book and its prior-work penalty. The actual result depends on the enchantments and order; do not treat a balanced tree as an automatic minimum.

Use the optimizer

  1. Open the Enchantment Optimizer and select your item.
  2. Enter the item's prior-work count and select the distinct fresh books and levels you have.
  3. Calculate and follow the ordered left and right inputs in each step, checking the anvil cost in game.
  4. Stop if an enchantment is incompatible, a displayed cost differs, or an operation exceeds the survival anvil limit.

The model supports up to eight distinct enchantment books with no prior work; existing enchantments and other anvil operations are not fully modeled. A level total is not a raw XP-point total because levels cost different amounts of XP. See the bulk crafting guide for material planning and Minecraft Wiki: Anvil mechanics for further details.
